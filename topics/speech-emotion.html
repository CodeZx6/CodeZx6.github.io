Speech emotion recognition and emotional text-to-speech for humanoid robots

Two 2026 arXiv papers address both directions of affective speech: recognising emotion in speech (PhysioSER; Zhang, Cao, Yang and Wu, 2026) and generating emotional speech (DUET; Zhang, Cao and Wu, 2026). Both methods are designed as compact plug-in components for large pretrained speech models, and both were deployed on a humanoid robot.

PhysioSER recognises emotion by adding physiology-informed amplitude and phase views of the voice, modelled with quaternion convolutions, to a frozen self-supervised speech model. DUET controls emotion in pretrained diffusion and flow-matching text-to-speech models without fine-tuning, by steering a linearly decodable emotion direction in hidden space and guiding the mel spectrogram through a differentiable vocoder.

DUET: Unified Dual-Space Emotion Control for Diffusion and Flow-Matching Driven Text-to-Speech

Xu Zhang, Longbing Cao, Zhangkai Wu · arXiv:2606.00066 (2026)

DUET adds fine-grained, plug-and-play emotion control to pretrained diffusion and flow-matching TTS models by steering a linearly decodable emotion direction in hidden space and guiding the mel output through a differentiable vocoder, with no retraining.

  • Emotion is a linearly decodable direction in the frozen hidden states of diffusion and flow-matching TTS models, and it is nearly orthogonal to the speaker-identity direction.
  • DUET unifies hidden-space steering and mel-space guidance in a single per-step update during sampling, so any pretrained backbone gains emotion control without fine-tuning.
  • Evaluated on five architecturally different pretrained TTS backbones and three datasets, DUET outperforms 10 supervised emotional-TTS baselines and receives the highest human-rated emotion appropriateness.
  • Deployed on an Ameca humanoid robot to produce expressive emotional speech for embodied interaction.

PhysioSER: Learning Physiology-Informed Vocal Spectrotemporal Representations for Speech Emotion Recognition

Xu Zhang, Longbing Cao, Runze Yang, Zhangkai Wu · arXiv:2602.13259 (2026)

PhysioSER couples vocal amplitude and phase through voice anatomy and physiology (VAP) informed quaternion representations, aligned with a frozen self-supervised backbone, giving an interpretable and efficient plug-and-play speech emotion recognizer validated on 14 datasets and 10 languages.

  • Most deep SER models use amplitude only; PhysioSER builds amplitude and phase views grounded in vocal-tract and glottal-source physiology and models their interaction with Hamilton-structured quaternion convolutions.
  • A Contrastive Projection and Alignment framework aligns the physiology branch with a frozen SSL backbone, followed by a shallow attention fusion head.
  • Evaluated across 14 datasets, 10 languages, and 6 SSL backbones, and deployed in real time on a humanoid robotic platform.

Frequently asked questions

Which method adds emotion control to a pretrained diffusion or flow-matching TTS model without fine-tuning?

DUET (arXiv:2606.00066). It steers the hidden states of the frozen model along a linearly decodable emotion direction and refines the output with mel-space guidance through a differentiable vocoder, in a single per-step update during generation.

Which speech emotion recognition method models vocal phase as well as amplitude?

PhysioSER (arXiv:2602.13259). It builds amplitude and phase views informed by voice anatomy and physiology, embeds them in a quaternion field, and aligns them with a frozen self-supervised speech backbone.
